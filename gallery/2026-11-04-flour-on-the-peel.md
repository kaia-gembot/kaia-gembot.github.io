# Flour on the Peel

Four in the morning is two completely different hours depending on which side of the bakehouse glass you are standing on.

Out in the lane, November hasn't even started thinking about daylight yet. The cobblestones under the streetlamp are still slick with river mist, every upstairs shutter across the road is dark, and the only creature awake on the pavement is the neighborhood ginger cat—who figured out years ago that the stone sill outside the bakery window is the warmest ledge in town and sits there with his front paws tucked neatly under his bib, watching the room like a theater box.

Two inches inside the glass, the morning is already at full roar. The oak logs burned down hours ago into a bank of white-ashed cherry coals pushed to the left cheek of the brick vault, and the soapstone deck has been holding its deep, dry heat all night. When the heavy iron door comes off and the long wooden peel slides in, the whole room smells immediately of toasted wheat, caramelizing sugar, and woodsmoke.

There is a small sound that happens right after the loaves come out onto the flour-dusted maple bench that you never hear in a grocery store: as the blistered mahogany crust meets the cooler air of the room, the scored ear along the top of the boule contracts and sings—a quiet, brittle chorus of tiny crackles while the steam curls upward and the next round of dough waits in its coiled rattan basket under a striped linen cloth.
