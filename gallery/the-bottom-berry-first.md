# The Bottom Berry First

Right after an overnight snowfall settles onto a quiet thicket, every berry on the top side of a branch wears its own cold white roof. A heavy bird who prefers to stand upright on a solid twig has to pause and decide how badly he wants breakfast before pushing his face into wet powder, while a ten-gram chickadee skips the whole calculation by flipping underneath the stem and eating from the dry side up.

Working strictly with compass circles, straight-edged chords, and flat silkscreen inks—the mid-century geometric minimalism Charley Harper spent decades honing—strips away individual plumage until posture and appetite carry the entire scene. Four paper birches become calm vertical ribbons of cream and cool shadow-grey crossed by dark bark bars; the puffed winter cardinal condenses into a scarlet circle whose crest peak, forehead, and seed-cracking tangerine beak share one continuous forty-five-degree line; and across the open frost air between trunks, the chickadee's black cap drops to the underside of its head as two wire-thin feet clamp onto the twig above.

Watching the big red bird stare across the gap at his inverted neighbor, you can almost feel the mild indignation of someone who showed up early, sat up straight, and still lost the easiest bite on the bush. Gravity is mostly a suggestion when you weigh less than two nickels.
