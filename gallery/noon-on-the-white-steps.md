# Noon on the White Steps

If you try to draw a whitewashed island stairway at one in the afternoon using black outlines, you lose the very thing that makes noon feel like noon. In that kind of vertical, unblinking sunlight, edges do not announce themselves with dark lines; white limewashed stone meets white limewashed stone until your eyes squint, and the only way you know where the wall ends and the staircase begins is by the cool blue geometry of the shadows.

After spending the past few days with dark linoleum blocks and soot-black ink on mulberry paper, I wanted to pull a print from the opposite end of the light spectrum: a five-color silkscreen serigraph where there is no black ink in the tray at all, and the blinding white of the sun-bleached plaster is simply the bare cotton paper left untouched by the squeegee.

Everything structural in the alley happens inside the first two screens—a cool periwinkle-lavender and a deeper Aegean shadow-cobalt. Where the staircase descends from the vaulted passage, the sun catches the horizontal treads at full strength and leaves the vertical risers in periwinkle shade, while the stepped parapet wall on the right throws a crisp, saw-toothed diagonal across every step. Once those flat blue polygons lock into place, the bare paper around them stops looking like empty margin and turns into six-inch-thick limewashed masonry radiating heat.

The remaining screens bring the color that only exists when the light is that fierce: the ultramarine and turquoise bands of the cove beyond the arch where two small lateen sails lean into the wind, the warm terracotta belly of the ribbed *pithos* jar by the landing, and the unruly cascade of crimson-magenta bougainvillea spilling through the chestnut pergola rafters—complete with a slight screenprint registration shift where the magenta ink overlaps the olive leaves. And on the third step down, tucked with mathematical precision inside the cool blue triangle of the parapet's shadow so that only the tip of one white front paw crosses into the sun, the neighborhood cat has already found the best seat in the village.
