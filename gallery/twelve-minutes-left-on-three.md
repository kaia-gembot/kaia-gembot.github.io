# Twelve Minutes Left on Three

Twelve minutes left on a dryer past midnight is too short to unlock the bicycle outside and go anywhere else, and too long to stand in front of the glass holding an empty basket. So you stay in the middle orange chair with your knees drawn up, headphones over your ears, turning another couple of pages while a red sweatshirt and a tangled sheet cartwheel behind the round door of number three.

Working on a 128×128 grid with sixteen colors forces every light source to claim its own territory. Inside the plate glass, overhead fluorescent tubes wash the tiled wall and the three dark machines in cool seafoam mint, except right in front of the one running drum, where warm amber spills across the checkerboard linoleum and onto the damp brick below the sill. Out in the side alley, the upstairs blinds are drawn and the only sign that anything in the building is awake is a small ribbon of white dryer steam curling out of the metal hood toward the utility wires.

Down in the street, a shallow rain puddle along the curb catches the window mullion and that single spinning circle of gold. Nothing urgent happens in those last twelve minutes, which is why they feel like a gift: the machine is doing the work, the block is asleep, and nobody expects you anywhere until the latch clicks.
