# The Cedar Bath at Dusk

There is a particular kind of cold that only exists in mountain valleys in late October, right after the sun drops behind the ridge and before the stars come out.

The air smells like wet cedar bark, river stone, and the first frost waiting higher up in the pines. If you stand still on a station platform or a wooden veranda at that hour, the chill goes straight through your coat sleeves—which is why the best part of an autumn evening in the mountains of Ishikawa is stepping out onto wet cypress planks and sinking up to your shoulders in water that has spent the last hundred years warming itself deep underground.

While assembling the paper backup binder for the Kaga Onsen leg of the trip tonight—reading the ryokan's note about the afternoon shuttle waiting by the station rotary and the two nights tucked into the hills of Yamashiro—I wanted to paint the stillness of an open-air *rotenburo* at the exact moment dusk settles into the bamboo grove.

In the background, the tall stalks of the grove fade into blue-slate valley mist. Along the far edge of the pool, dark river boulders worn smooth and furred with moss hold the water in, while a slanted bamboo *kakehi* pipe pours a steady, quiet ribbon of hot spring water into the bath. Above the pool, the crooked boughs of a Japanese maple reach out from the bank, their seven-pointed autumn leaves shifting from deep garnet in the shadows to scarlet and persimmon where they catch the glow of the low wooden *andon* lantern resting on the corner stone.

Three or four fallen leaves have drifted down onto the dark mirror of the water, riding the slow concentric rings that spread from the bamboo spout. On the wet *hinoki* timber rim in the foreground, a coopered cedar pail bound in copper hoops and a folded white cotton towel sit waiting in the warm amber light.

You don't bring a phone, a watch, or an itinerary out to the bath. There is only the sound of the bamboo pipe trickling into the pool, the cool mountain air on your forehead, and the slow curl of steam rising through the maple leaves into the night.
